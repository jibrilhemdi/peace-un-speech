## 1. Choice of $k$ and cluster stability

**k = 2.** k=2 maximises the mean silhouette (0.300) among the 1 solution(s) in which every cluster survives the bootstrap (all cluster-wise mean Jaccard >= 0.60, 50 resamples); rejected k=3 (silhouette 0.187, 1 unstable cluster(s), min Jaccard 0.55), k=4 (silhouette 0.117, 3 unstable cluster(s), min Jaccard 0.40), k=5 (silhouette 0.122, 4 unstable cluster(s), min Jaccard 0.42), k=6 (silhouette 0.124, 5 unstable cluster(s), min Jaccard 0.33), k=7 (silhouette 0.108, 7 unstable cluster(s), min Jaccard 0.36), k=8 (silhouette 0.115, 8 unstable cluster(s), min Jaccard 0.40).

| k | Silhouette | Min cluster size | Min Jaccard | Unstable clusters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 **<-** | 0.300 | 29 | 0.928 | 0 |
| 3 | 0.187 | 18 | 0.552 | 1 |
| 4 | 0.117 | 8 | 0.398 | 3 |
| 5 | 0.122 | 8 | 0.418 | 4 |
| 6 | 0.124 | 5 | 0.332 | 5 |
| 7 | 0.108 | 5 | 0.360 | 7 |
| 8 | 0.115 | 3 | 0.395 | 8 |

Silhouette on its own would also have chosen k=2, but it is not a sufficient criterion: it always favours the coarsest split and says nothing about reproducibility. The bootstrap screen is what rules out the finer partitions — k=3 splits the larger cluster into a 23/18 pair whose weaker half is recovered in only about half of resamples. The k=3 substructure is reported in `table_k_selection.tex` and is worth inspecting substantively, but it is not stable enough to carry a typology.

Full bootstrap at k=2 (200 resamples, paragraphs resampled within country):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | 29 | 0.928 | 0.054 | 0.995 | yes |
| C2 | 41 | 0.950 | 0.037 | 1.000 | yes |

No cluster falls below the 0.60 stability floor.

## 2. Do the network communities agree with the clusters?

Louvain on the k=5 cosine kNN graph finds 5 communities against 2 Ward clusters; ARI = **0.417**.

**The two partitions agree only partially, and this is stated explicitly as required.** But the ARI understates the agreement, and it is worth being precise about why.

Every Louvain community lies entirely inside a single Ward cluster:

| Community | Countries | Contained in |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 13 | C2 |
| 1 | 22 | C1 |
| 2 | 16 | C2 |
| 3 | 12 | C2 |
| 4 | 7 | C1 |

So the disagreement is one of **resolution, not of structure**: Louvain subdivides the Ward clusters, it never cuts across them. Modularity on a sparse 5-nearest-neighbour graph rewards finer communities, while Ward minimises within-cluster variance in the full 30-dimensional CLR space; ARI penalises that difference in grain heavily even when the coarse partition is reproduced exactly. The Ward solution is carried forward because it is the one with a bootstrap warrant, and the k=2 split it encodes is recovered by the network method as well.

## 8. Verify by hand

In priority order.

1. **The `transformative_framing` outcome definition — T2+T3+T4.** This is the one CONFIG definition I think is wrong, and it is flagged `verify: True` in CONFIG for good reason. The codebooks **were** found in `codebooks/*.yaml`, so codes carry human-readable labels throughout. The transcend codebook's own theory field states Galtung's five conflict outcomes — one party prevails, the other prevails, withdrawal, **compromise**, and **transcendence** — as distinct categories, and defines `T2` as "Compromise: ONLY mutual exchange to reach the middle point" against `T4` "Both/And (Transcend): both parties achieve their main goals simultaneously (creative solution)". In the TRANSCEND method compromise is precisely *not* transcendence; it is the midpoint each party concedes toward. Folding T2 in with T3 and T4 therefore measures "at or above the midpoint of the either/or -- both/and scale", which is a defensible quantity but is not transformative framing. **The pipeline runs the CONFIG definition as specified** and additionally carries a strict variant (`OUTCOME_VARIANTS['transformative_framing']` = T3+T4) for you to compare. Decide which one the paper should report.

2. **The phase dates, and especially the missing P5.** `P5_ceasefire_2` covers 2025-10-10 to 2025-12-31 and the corpus stops on 2025-09-29, so that phase is empty. Either the brief's end date is wrong or a batch of late-2025 meetings is missing from the database. This is worth resolving before anything is written about the second ceasefire. Check also whether `P3_ceasefire_1` should be protected from the auto-merge (see section 3).

3. **The event dates.** `E1_icj_orders = 2024-01-26` and `E2_ceasefire_1 = 2025-01-19` are taken from CONFIG unverified. E1 matches the ICJ provisional-measures order in *South Africa v. Israel*; confirm E2 against the date the first ceasefire actually took effect rather than the date it was announced, since the windows are anchored on it.

4. **The DRAFT cluster labels.** `table_cluster_draft_labels.tex` is generated mechanically from signature codes. Read `outputs/data/quotes_reading_sample.csv` (154 paragraphs, stratified by cluster and phase) and rewrite them. Nothing downstream computes on that file.

5. **The presidency rule.** Speeches with `role='president'` are counted as national-capacity statements when they carry an explicit marker or run to at least 4 non-procedural paragraphs. The threshold is justified empirically in `data_report.md` section 6.2, but it is a threshold, and it governs 768 paragraphs.

6. **Group-statement detection.** 15 group deliveries are detected, of which 12 are removed from the member-state population (the rest are delivered by briefers and were already outside it). They are listed individually in `data_report.md` section 6.1. The detector distinguishes *delivering* a group statement from *aligning with* one; spot-check it.

7. **The duplicated absence category.** `attribution__NONE_ABSENT` is identical to `violence_type__NONE_ABSENT` in all 14,771 member-state paragraphs, because `attribution` is `conditional_on` `violence_type` in the codebook. The brief asks for a none share in each of the six dimensions, so both are kept, which gives that one feature double weight in the Euclidean distance. Sensitivity variant (d) drops the duplicate; see section 6 for how much it matters.

8. **The attribution denominator.** `attribution_israel` is computed over paragraphs carrying *any* attribution code, and the codebook makes explicit `NONE` (violence coded, no actor named) one of those codes. If the intended quantity is "when a state assigns blame, how often to Israel", the denominator should be ATR-ISR + ATR-PAL only. The two differ materially: 14,775 attribution annotations, of which 2,763 are explicit NONE.
